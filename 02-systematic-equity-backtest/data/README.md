# Data card — S&P 500 daily OHLCV, 2013–2018

Every result in this project inherits the defects of this file, so the file gets
audited before any strategy touches it. Everything below is **generated by
`scripts/explore_data.py`** — the numbers, the integrity verdicts and the row
listings are reproducible, not hand-written.

> Run it yourself: `python scripts/explore_data.py --data data/all_stocks_5yr.csv`
> → `reports/figures/01_data_overview.png`, `reports/tables/data_*.csv`

## 2. Schema

Long / tidy format, one row per stock-day:

| column | type | description | nulls |
|---|---|---|---|
| `date` | date (`YYYY-MM-DD`) | trading date | 0 |
| `open` | float | opening price, USD | 11 |
| `high` | float | session high, USD | 8 |
| `low` | float | session low, USD | 8 |
| `close` | float | closing price, USD | 0 |
| `volume` | int | shares traded | 0 |
| `Name` | string | ticker symbol | 0 |

Prices are **as-traded, not total-return**: no dividend adjustment, and — as §4
shows — no adjustment for splits or spin-offs either. Only `close` is used
downstream; `open`/`high`/`low` feed the integrity checks.

### The failing rows

`reports/tables/data_integrity_violations.csv` lists all **20** defective rows —
9 with mutually inconsistent prices (AOS trips both price checks, which is why
the counts above sum to 10) and 11 with a missing OHLC field. The worst:

| date | ticker | open | high | low | close | problem |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2016-05-19 | LNT | 35.19 | 35.79 | 35.09 | **17.87** | close is half the session low |
| 2014-05-19 | AOS | 23.89 | **23.60** | 23.85 | 24.45 | high below low *and* below close |
| 2014-05-28 | SPG | 176.23 | 176.60 | 174.50 | **165.42** | close 5% below the low |
| 2014-06-30 | CHK | 30.43 | 31.10 | 30.37 | **29.39** | close below the low |
| 2016-05-13 | WRK | 42.45 | 42.89 | 41.82 | **38.39** | close below the low |

The LNT row is the one that actually propagates: a bad close of 17.87 on
2016-05-19 manufactures a **+100.9% "return"** on 2016-05-20 when the price
"recovers" to 35.91. That single bad print is the largest return in the entire
panel.

### Unadjusted corporate actions

`reports/tables/extreme_moves.csv` lists every day-over-day move beyond ±50%:

| date | ticker | prev close | close | "return" | what it actually is |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015-07-02 | NI | 45.45 | 16.99 | −62.6% | Columbia Pipeline Group spin-off, effective 2015-07-01 |
| 2015-07-20 | EBAY | 66.29 | 28.57 | −56.9% | PayPal separation, ex-distribution 2015-07-20 |
| 2014-08-07 | DISCK | 81.22 | 39.78 | −51.0% | 2-for-1 split, ex-date 2014-08-07 |
| 2014-08-07 | DISCA | 83.02 | 40.98 | −50.6% | 2-for-1 split, ex-date 2014-08-07 |
| 2013-04-19 | VRTX | 52.87 | 85.60 | +61.9% | genuine news-driven move |
| 2016-04-22 | AMD | 2.62 | 3.99 | +52.3% | genuine earnings move |
| 2016-05-20 | LNT | 17.87 | 35.91 | +100.9% | artefact of the bad print above |

**Four of seven are not returns at all** — they are corporate actions the vendor
never adjusted for. This matters directly: a short-term-reversal signal sees
eBay "fall 57%" and buys it enthusiastically, and a momentum signal carries the
fake drop in its 12-month lookback for a year afterwards.

**These rows are left in.** The strategies run on the data as distributed, which
is the honest baseline; documenting the contamination and quantifying its size
is more useful than silently patching seven rows and reporting a cleaner-looking
number. Removing them is the first item on the extension list in the project
README.

## 5. Known biases

**Survivorship bias — the dominant one.** The universe is the set of firms in the
S&P 500 at the *end* of the window. Companies removed from the index during
2013–2018 (through bankruptcy, acquisition or relegation) are simply absent.
Panel (a) of `reports/figures/01_data_overview.png` shows the fingerprint:
listed names rise monotonically from 476 to 505 and **never fall**, which no real
index membership ever does.

Consequence: the equal-weight benchmark's Sharpe of 1.09 is materially
overstated. The market-neutral long/short books are much less exposed, since they
are short the same biased universe they are long — which is exactly why they look
worse in a naive side-by-side comparison.

**No point-in-time membership.** Because the file has no join/leave dates, the
same universe is used throughout, which also means the earliest dates include
firms that were not yet in the index.

**No adjustment for splits, spin-offs or dividends.** See §4. Total returns are
understated by the dividend yield (~2%/yr over this period), and corporate
actions inject spurious returns.

**Large-cap US only, one bull market.** 2013–2018 contains no sustained bear
market. Any conclusion about strategy robustness across regimes is out of sample.

## 7. Preprocessing applied downstream

Implemented in `src/quantbt/data.py`, in order:

1. Parse `date`, sort by `(Name, date)`.
2. Pivot long → wide (`to_price_matrix`): index = date, columns = ticker.
3. **Drop tickers present for < 98% of trading days** — 505 → 476. Removes names
   that listed or delisted mid-sample and would otherwise inject look-ahead into
   the cross-section.
4. Forward-fill remaining gaps, then drop any date still incomplete.
5. Convert to simple daily returns (`to_returns`).

Every step is covered by a test in `tests/test_quantbt.py`.
